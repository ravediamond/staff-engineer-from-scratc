# 1. What changes at principal

## Scope and horizon

Staff engineers own a problem space and its systems over quarters. Principals own a **technical strategy**
over years: which platforms the company builds or buys, which bets it makes (e.g. "every AI product ships
behind a common evaluation and harness platform"), which risks it takes. The work is less "design the system"
and more "decide which systems should exist, and make sure the org can build them without me".

## The four archetypes

| Archetype | Example in your domain | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technical direction | the company's AI platform strategy: harness, evaluation, data, serving | multi-year roadmap adopted, teams aligned to it, measurable outcomes |
| Deep expert | the reference on agent evaluation and reward signals | the hardest problems routed to you, external recognition |
| Crisis solver | the AI product that keeps failing in production, fixed | turnaround metrics, the systemic fixes that stuck |
| Industry presence | open-source eval/agent tooling and writing that the field uses | adoption, citations, invitations, hiring pull |

Most principals are strong in one or two and credible in the rest.

## Failure modes

- **Astronaut architecture:** strategy disconnected from code and production reality. Antidote: stay hands-on in
  prototypes, reviews and incidents for ~20–30% of your time.
- **Hero mode:** solving everything yourself. Antidote: measure yourself by what others ship on your foundations.
- **Strategy without decisions:** documents that describe trends but commit to nothing. Antidote: every strategy
  names what you will **not** do.
- **Influence without trust:** pushing direction before having been right in public a few times. Antidote: small,
  visible, correct calls first.
