# 15. From staff to principal

Short version of `principal/README.md`; read that for the playbook and exercises.

## What changes

| | Staff | Principal |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of ownership | a problem space across teams | a technical strategy for an org or company |
| Time horizon | quarters to a year | years |
| Main output | designs, platforms, unblocked teams | bets, direction, an org that decides better |
| Judged on | outcomes of the systems you shaped | whether the bets were right, and the leverage created |
| Failure mode | heroics, staying too close to code | astronaut architecture, losing technical credibility |

## The four ways principals create value (pick your archetype, then grow the others)

1. **Technical direction:** a multi-year architecture or platform strategy the org follows.
2. **Deep expert:** the person the company calls for the hardest problems in one domain (evaluation, distributed training).
3. **Solver of existential problems:** parachuted into the crisis that matters most this year.
4. **Industry presence:** shapes the field (standards, open source, papers) in ways that bring advantage back.

## Questions

1. What would make you principal-level in evaluation infrastructure?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A company-wide evaluation strategy you authored and that many teams follow; an eval platform and governance that changed how launch decisions are made (offline-online agreement tracked, regressions near zero); recognized external influence (open-source tools, talks, standards for agent evaluation); and a bench of engineers you grew who now own parts of it.</details>
