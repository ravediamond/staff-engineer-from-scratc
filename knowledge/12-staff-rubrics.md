# 12. What staff means at Google, Meta and Paris unicorns

Public information only; loops change. Ask your recruiter for the current format and calibrate with people who
interviewed recently.

## §1 Google (L6 Staff Software Engineer)

Typical loop: ~2 coding, 1–2 system design (ML system design for ML roles), 1 behavioral/leadership
("Googleyness and leadership"), sometimes a domain deep dive. Hiring committee reviews written feedback, so what
the interviewer can **write down** matters: clear reasoning, trade-offs, quantified impact. Attributes commonly
named in public material: general cognitive ability, role-related knowledge, leadership, and "Googleyness"
(collaboration, comfort with ambiguity, doing the right thing). Team matching happens after the committee.

## §2 Meta (E6)

Typical loop: 2 coding (often fast-paced, two problems per round), 1–2 system design (product design or
infra design; ML system design for ML roles), 1 behavioral that weighs heavily on leveling. Behavioral probes
scope, conflict resolution, growing others, and driving direction. Meta's performance axes (public descriptions:
project impact, direction, engineering excellence, people) are a good map for stories.

## §3 Paris unicorns and scale-ups (Mistral, Hugging Face, H, Dust, Photoroom, Poolside, Alan, Doctolib…)

Formats vary more: take-homes or pair programming on realistic code, deep technical discussions with founders or
senior researchers, system design grounded in their product, and strong weight on **shipping** and ownership
("founding-staff energy"). Public artifacts (OSS contributions, writing, a visible project) count more than at
big tech. Expect questions about why you, why them, why now, and compensation in BSPCE/equity terms.

## §4 Judgment habits that read as staff in any loop

1. **Prove it works.** Evidence before claims: tests, evals with CIs, measured outcomes.
2. **Fix root causes.** The systemic fix and the org change, not just the patch.
3. **Write it down.** Design docs with non-goals and alternatives; decision records.
4. **Quantify.** Numbers before architecture; cost as a first-class requirement.
5. **Choose the boring path on purpose.** Buy or reuse undifferentiated parts.
6. **Make others faster.** Platforms, templates, reviews, mentoring, with adoption metrics.
7. **Say "I don't know" precisely** and how you would find out.

## §5 Migrations and operating at scale

Dual-write, backfill with versions, verify, gated cutover, rollback until old writes stop; client inventories from
logs; deprecation timelines with dashboards. The same discipline applies to model migrations and embedding changes.
