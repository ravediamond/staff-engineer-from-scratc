# Questions

1. Write the HPA formula. What are tolerance and the stabilization window for?
2. HPA vs VPA vs KEDA vs Cluster Autoscaler/Karpenter: what does each scale, on what signal?
3. Liveness, readiness and startup probes: what does each do on failure, and the classic misconfiguration of each?
4. Choose maxSurge and maxUnavailable for a 10-replica GPU inference service with no spare GPUs. Justify.
5. Rolling vs blue-green vs canary: mechanics, cost and when to use each. What does Argo Rollouts add?
6. What does a PodDisruptionBudget protect against and not? How can it block a cluster upgrade?

## Model answers

1. desired = ceil(current × currentMetric / targetMetric), clamped to [min, max]. Tolerance (10%): ignore small deviations so replicas do not flap around the target. Stabilization window: scale-down applies the highest recommendation of the last 5 minutes (configurable per direction, with rate policies), because removing capacity on a momentary dip and re-adding it costs more than waiting; scale-up is immediate by default.
2. HPA: replica count, on CPU/memory or custom/external metrics. VPA: the requests of each pod (restarts pods unless in-place resize is used), on observed usage: for workloads that cannot scale out. KEDA: feeds HPA with event-source metrics (queue length, Kafka lag, Prometheus queries) and scales to and from zero. Cluster Autoscaler/Karpenter: nodes, on unschedulable pods and under-used nodes. They compose: KEDA/HPA create pods, the node autoscaler makes room.
3. Liveness: restart the container when it fails; misuse: checking a dependency (database down → every pod restarts in a loop) or too short a timeout under load. Readiness: remove the pod from Service endpoints while failing, no restart; misuse: none defined (traffic to pods that are still loading), or a check so strict that all pods go unready together. Startup: holds off the other probes until the app has started; misuse: not using it for slow starters (model loading) and instead setting a huge liveness delay, which also delays detection of real hangs.
4. No spare GPUs means a surge pod cannot be scheduled: `maxSurge: 0, maxUnavailable: 1` (replace one at a time, capacity dips by 10% during the rollout, so roll at low traffic and keep 10% headroom), with a startup probe covering model load and graceful drain of in-flight streams (`terminationGracePeriodSeconds`, preStop). If one spare GPU exists: `maxSurge: 1, maxUnavailable: 0` keeps full capacity.
5. Rolling: replace pods gradually in place; cheap, both versions serve during the rollout, rollback = another rollout. Blue-green: run the full new stack next to the old, switch traffic at once; instant rollback, double capacity (painful on GPUs), needs compatible schemas. Canary: send a small share of traffic to the new version, compare metrics, increase stepwise; best risk control, needs traffic splitting and good metrics. Argo Rollouts (or Flagger) automates canary/blue-green: traffic weights through the mesh or gateway, metric analysis from Prometheus, automatic promotion or abort.
6. It limits VOLUNTARY evictions (drain, upgrades, autoscaler consolidation) so that at least minAvailable (or at most maxUnavailable) pods of the app are affected at once. It does nothing for crashes, node failures or a Deployment's own rollout. A PDB that can never be satisfied (minAvailable equal to replicas, or a single replica with minAvailable 1) makes every drain of its node hang, which stalls node upgrades; platform teams alert on such PDBs or set drain timeouts.
